You know, if you take people — and I've told you this — and you expose them voluntarily to things that they're avoiding and are afraid of in order to meet their goals, their self-defined goals, if you can teach people to stand up in the face of the things they are afraid of, they get stronger.

You don't know what the upper limits to that are. You might ask yourself: if over 10 years you didn't avoid doing what you knew you needed to do, by your own definitions, within the structure that you've created, to the degree that you've done that, what would you be like?

Well, there are remarkable people, and they're people who do find out over decades-long periods what they could be like if they were who they are. And they get stronger and stronger and stronger. We don't know the limits to that.

And so you could say, well, part of the reason that your suffering unbearably can be left at your feet is because you're not everything you could be, and you know it. And of course that's a terrible thing to admit, and it's a terrible thing to consider. But there's real promise in it, right?

Because it means that perhaps there's another way that you could look at the world, and another way that you could act in the world, so what it would reflect back to you would be much better than what it reflects back to you now.

Because, you know, my experience is with people: we're probably running at about 51% of our capacity. If I can learn anything about this, it's that there are undergraduates, and they ask themselves, "How many hours a week do you waste?"

Of course, a classic is something like 4 to 6 hours a day. You know, inefficient studying, watching things on YouTube — not only do you not want to watch, but that make you feel horrible about watching after you're done. That's probably 4 hours right there.

Now you think, well, that's 20-25 hours a week, 2.5 full work weeks a month, that's 10 work weeks per year. $20 an hour is a radical underestimate if you think about it in terms of deferred wages. If you're wasting 20 hours a week, you're wasting $50,000 a year.

And it's because you're young. Wasting $50,000 a year is a way bigger catastrophe than it would be for me to waste it. I'm not going to last nearly as long.

And so, if your life isn't everything it could be, what would happen if you just stopped wasting the opportunities that are right in front of you? You'd be... who knows how much more efficient — 10x more efficient, 20x more efficient.

That's the Pareto distribution. You have no idea how efficient efficient people get. It's completely off the charts.

And so, you know, you think: well, there's 7 billion people in the world, and you're just one little dust mote among that 7 billion. It really doesn't matter what you do or don't do.

But that's simply not the case. You're at the center of a network. You're a node in a network. Of course, that's even more true now that we have social media. You'll know a thousand people, and that puts you one person away from a million, and two persons away from a billion.

And so that's how you're connected. And the things you do, they're like dropping a stone in a pond: the ripples move outwards, and they affect things in ways that you can scarcely comprehend.

And it means that the things that you do and the things that you don't do are far more important than you think.
